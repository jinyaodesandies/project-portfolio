Carney Summer Scholars · SREIP for VLANS

Rush: A 3D Computational Fluid Model of Neuro-Vascular Coupling

A mechanism for heat management and biochemical computing

Working with Christopher I. Moore at the Carney Center for Brain Science, I built a 3D model of a neural–capillary system using the open-source meshing software SALOME and the computational fluid dynamics solver OpenFOAM. The model simulates temperature and fluid transfer in the brain to explore how neuro-vascular coupling could support heat management and chemical signaling between neurons.

Funded by the National Institute of Neurological Disorders and Stroke through the Carney Summer Scholars Program.

Computational Neuroscience

How real brains store, manage, and let go of memory — the analytical counterpart to the featured Rush model above.

NEUR 122 · Final Paper · December 2025

Anticipating Memory Lapses within the Chickadee’s Hippocampal Barcoding

Chickadees remember thousands of food caches, and their hippocampus stamps each caching episode with a unique “barcode” of neural activity. Reanalyzing the electrophysiology dataset behind Chettih et al.’s barcoding discovery, we compressed the firing of caching-related neurons into a signature for each cache, paired every signature with its timestamp, and measured how signatures from the same location drift apart over time — evidence that the brain actively loosens episode-specific codes to free neurons for new memories.

Machine Learning & Games

Building a game — and agents that learn to play it the way people do.

Independent Research · Human & Machine Learning

Expanding Hexxed: Giving a Game-Playing Agent Spatial Intuition

Hexxed is a cross-platform Unity mini-game built to compare how humans and machine agents learn: players grow each lobe of a hexagon to exactly full size before collecting it, a task that maps cleanly onto a Markov decision process. The research asks why deep Q-learning agents lag behind human intuition — and tests whether a custom convolutional network, which sees the board as a spatial grid instead of a flattened vector, earns rewards more efficiently by exploiting adjacency the way people naturally do.
